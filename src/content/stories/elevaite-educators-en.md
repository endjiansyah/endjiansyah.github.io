Introducing artificial intelligence literacy and computational thinking into classrooms begins with empowering those on the frontlines: our educators. Between **November 2025 and September 2026**, the **Practical AI & Coding Skills for Educators** program—held under **Microsoft Elevate**, led by **Alkademi**, and supported by **ParagonCorp**—served as an expansive collaborative hub connecting thousands of passionate teachers across Indonesia.

Within this program, there was never a notion of a "lead instructor" or rigid hierarchy. We upheld the core principle that **all educators and facilitators stand on equal footing**. Whether gathered in-person or across online video rooms, every session functioned as a collaborative circle to exchange real-world classroom challenges, demystify technology, and co-create engaging ways to deliver computational thinking to young learners.

---

### Sustained Hybrid and Routine Cohorts

The initiative was designed with recurring, adaptive cohorts. Given the immense geography of the Indonesian archipelago spanning three distinct time zones (WIB, WITA, and WIT), the curriculum blended intensive online sessions with hands-on offline workshops and hybrid setups at partner educational institutions.

![Hybrid Microsoft Elevate training session with educators at SMKN 1 Kaligondang Purbalingga](/assets/img/stories/elevaite-educators/smkn1-kaligondang-purbalingga-hybrid.webp)

Teachers experienced hands-on game-based learning through **Hour of Code** (powered by *Minecraft Education*) and learned foundational AI ethics and classification mechanisms through **Hour of AI: AI for Oceans**.

### Bridging Visual Blocks to Text-Based Logic

A common concern expressed by teachers from non-computer science disciplines was the belief that coding is inherently intimidating and full of convoluted syntax. Using an applied andragogical method, programming concepts were broken down through everyday analogies—contrasting *Block Coding* (visual drag-and-drop logic) with *Text Coding*.

![Endjiansyah breaking down Block Coding versus Text Coding in the computer lab of Universitas Muhammadiyah Bandung UMB](/assets/img/stories/elevaite-educators/umb-universitas-muhammadiyah-bandung-block-coding.webp)
*Endjiansyah detailing the conceptual transition from visual block programming to text coding in the computer lab of Universitas Muhammadiyah Bandung (UMB).*

By visualizing core logic structures such as loops, conditional statements (*if-else*), and directional sensors, teachers realized that the core of programming lies in clear systematic reasoning rather than rote syntax memorization.

### Unforgettable Moments: A Mosaic of Regional Perspectives

For me personally, the greatest reward from these months was discovering the rich **diversity of perspectives regarding technology from educators across different corners of Indonesia**.

Each community brought its own stories, local wisdom, and dedication:
- **Bu Liliana Tangu from Biak (Papua)** demonstrated inspiring perseverance in bringing *Minecraft Education* to students on an outer island, proving that geographic distance is no barrier to world-class digital aspiration.
- **Pak Nasir from Ambon (Maluku)** brought a heartwarming perspective on weaving computational logic into environmental awareness for local marine ecosystems.
- **Pak Raabiul Akbar from Makassar (South Sulawesi)** invigorated cohorts with creative ideas on harnessing AI to generate contextual, engaging learning aids for his students.
- **Bu Dewi from Purbalingga (Central Java)** alongside vocational teachers showcased tremendous enthusiasm in aligning secondary school curricula with modern technology standards.
