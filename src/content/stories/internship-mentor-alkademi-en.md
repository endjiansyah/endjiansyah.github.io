Serving as [**Project Manager (PM) at Alkademi**](/en/stories/project-manager-alkademi) encompassed a broad spectrum of responsibilities. Beyond orchestrating delivery timelines, product roadmaps, and sprint cadences for the core engineering team, I also held a deeply rewarding mandate: **serving as the Field Mentor and Technical Supervisor for vocational high school (SMK) interns and polytechnic undergraduates** hosted at Alkademi throughout 2024 and 2025.

Mentoring interns was never treated as a mere administrative chore or a matter of delegating menial work. At Alkademi, we believed that an internship is a vital launchpad where young talents develop the mental stamina, logical rigor, and collaborative work habits demanded by the modern software industry.

---

### A Phased Curriculum: Why Interns Don't Start on Core Tasks Right Away

A recurring question from interns upon their arrival was: *when do we start coding on Alkademi's live platforms?*

The answer was always **not immediately**. Throwing inexperienced interns directly into production repositories without a grounded mental model overwhelms them and creates unnecessary risk for product stability. Instead, we established a phased, deliberate learning track:

1. **Web Programming Fundamentals**: Solidifying core algorithmic thinking, modern web architecture, and clean code hygiene.
2. **Pixel-Perfect Slicing (Modern HTML & CSS)**: Honing visual acuity, layout responsiveness, and semantic markup to instill the habit of translating design specs into clean code.
3. **Modern Component Frameworks (Nuxt.js)**: Introducing single-page architecture, state management, and SSR/CSR paradigms via the Vue/Nuxt.js ecosystem.

### Stepping into QA First: Cultivating Developer Intuition and Empathy

Perhaps the most defining phase of their onboarding was serving as **Quality Assurance (QA) testers** before ever pushing code to core systems.

This deliberate pedagogical strategy served a vital purpose:
> *An exceptional software engineer must understand the complete user journey and possess an intuitive feel for how applications should genuinely behave and respond under diverse conditions.*

By walking in the shoes of a QA tester, interns learned to:
- Actively seek out edge cases and non-standard user journeys.
- Sharpen their sensibility toward user experience (UX), error states, and input validation.
- Author clear, structured, and reproducible bug reports for developers.

This experience naturally cultivated engineering empathy: when they eventually transitioned back to writing code, they no longer wrote software just to "make it pass," but engineered robust systems built with the end user in mind.

Once their architectural understanding and testing discipline matured, interns were invited to contribute directly to production tasks alongside Alkademi's core development team.

### Beyond Code: Engaging in Events and Non-Development Initiatives

A comprehensive vocational education extends beyond the laptop screen. In real industry environments, interpersonal communication, teamwork, and operational adaptability are every bit as critical as technical prowess.

For this reason, we regularly invited interns to step outside the office and assist on-the-ground operations when Alkademi hosted or supported major regional technology events.

The mentorship program spanned multiple educational tiers. In addition to vocational high school students like those from SMKN 1 Cisarua (2025), during 2024 I also mentored **undergraduate interns from Indramayu State Polytechnic (Politeknik Negeri Indramayu / Polindra)**.

### Bonds That Outlast the Clock: Hiking Tahura Forest

To me, mentorship is far more than a rigid teacher-student hierarchy. The healthiest learning environments thrive on mutual trust, warmth, and genuine camaraderie.

After weeks immersed in sprint backlogs, code reviews, and testing runs, we often carved out time together outside office hours. One highlight was a refreshing hiking trip to **Taman Hutan Raya (Tahura) Ir. H. Djuanda** in Dago Pakar, Bandung.

![Endjiansyah and SMKN 1 Cisarua interns hiking near the waterfall at Tahura Dago Bandung](/assets/img/stories/internship-mentor-alkademi/healing-tahura.webp)

Surrounded by lush forest and the roar of the waterfall, we shared candid conversations about their aspirations, career dreams, and life stories—free from office formalities. These shared experiences underscore that our internship program wasn't merely a temporary contract that ended with a certificate; it created enduring friendships and mentorship connections that remain strong to this day.
